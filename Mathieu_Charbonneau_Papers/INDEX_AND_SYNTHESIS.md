## 2. Key Conceptual Frameworks of Mathieu Charbonneau

### A. Modularity, Recombination, and the "Generative Recipe" (Charbonneau 2016)
- **The Failure of Scalar Cultural Evolution:** Traditional models treat cultural skill as an abstract scalar $z \in \mathbb{R}$ passed through high-fidelity copying. Charbonneau demonstrates that real technologies are **hierarchical generative recipes**: ordered sequences of operations and component modules.
- **Innovation by Recombination (Arthur 2009):** New technologies are rarely invented *ex nihilo*. Instead, two or more established technological modules are joined together.
- **The Interface Requirement:** Recombination cannot happen at random. To combine module $A$ and module $B$, an innovator must invent an **interface** that reconciles their structural and energetic constraints.
- **Cumulative Lineages:** A technological breakthrough is the creation of a new reusable module, which then becomes a building block for future combinatorial inventions.

### B. Taxonomy of Innovation Types (Charbonneau 2015)
Charbonneau distinguishes three distinct classes of cultural innovation:
1. **Parametric Refinement:** Fine-tuning continuous variables within an existing recipe (e.g., striking angle of a flint core, blade thickness, parameter tweaking).
2. **Modular Insertion/Deletion:** Adding a pre-existing step or omitting an unnecessary step from a recipe.
3. **Combinatorial Synthesis:** Creating an entirely new functional architecture by synthesizing disparate technological lineages.

### C. Open-Ended Cultural Evolution (Winters & Charbonneau 2025/2026)
- **Co-Evolution of Cultural System ($\mathbb{C}$) and Search Space ($\mathbb{S}$):**  
  Human technological history is open-ended because as the cultural inventory $\mathbb{C}$ expands, it actively alters the search space $\mathbb{S}$ (creating new problems, new needs, and new niches).
- **The Mathematical Dynamics:**  
  When adaptation is purely deterministic ($\eta, \lambda \to 1.0$), populations rapidly get stuck in local optima. Open-ended accumulation requires a balance where stochastic innovation shocks continually push the system into far-from-equilibrium states, while selection-like filtering preserves functional effectiveness.

## 3. Direct Application: Grounding in Patents & Technological Diversity

### Why Prof. Alexey Koshevoy Suggested This Framework
During his feedback, Prof. Koshevoy stated:
> *"Think about like a distribution that you can get. Maybe like distribution of number of patents... because this is kind of like the technological diversity... and then you can see like maybe number of patents vs number of patent citations... look at work by Mathieu Charbonneau... they were modeling precisely that... how innovations kind of determine the rate of change in patents."*

### How Charbonneau Directly Maps to Patents
1. **Patents as Modular Recipes:** Every patent contains claims structured as hierarchical recipes combining known prior art (citations) with a specific novel interface (the inventive step).
2. **Patent Citations as Cultural Lineages:**
   - When Patent $k$ cites prior Patents $\{p_1, p_2, \dots\}$, it represents the **recombination of existing modules** (Charbonneau 2016; Arthur 2009).
   - The citation distribution follows a **heavy-tailed power law / Yule distribution** (Bentley et al. 2004; Youn et al. 2015): a few foundational general-purpose technologies receive thousands of citations, while most receive few.
3. **The Agent Strategies in R&D:**
   - **Copiers (Exploiters):** Commercialize and reproduce existing high-citation patents without incurring R&D search costs.
   - **Innovators (Basic Science / Radical Inventors):** Pay high R&D search costs to generate novel patents by structural recombination.
   - **Adaptive Agents (Dynamic R&D Strategists):** Use reinforcement learning / Bayesian updating to toggle between licensing/copying proven patents during harsh economic climates ($H > 0$) and investing in novel patent recombination when returns are promising.
4. **Observable Metrics:**
   - **Technological Diversity:** Entropy or Gini coefficient across patent classification codes (IPC/CPC classes).
   - **Rate of Change in Patents ($\frac{dK}{dt}$):** How rapidly new patents are filed as a function of population size, harshness, and innovator share.
   - **Citation Skewness:** Power-law exponent $\alpha$ of the citation network over time.

## 4. Mathematical Blueprint for the Repertoire / Patent Extension

Following Winters & Charbonneau (2026) and Arthur (2009):

### Technology Repertoire $\mathbb{C}_t$
Let $\mathbb{C}_t = \{T_1, T_2, \dots, T_{K_t}\}$ be the collective technological repertoire at generation $t$, where each technology $T_k$ is a graph or bitstring representing its component modules:
$$T_k = (V_k, E_k)$$
where $V_k \subset \mathcal{M}$ is the set of primitive/composite modules, and $E_k$ represents the interface links between them.

### Payoff with Combinatorial Depth
The cultural complexity (and resulting productivity) of technology $T_k$ scales super-linearly with its modular depth $|V_k|$:
$$z(T_k) = z_0 \cdot |V_k|^\gamma, \quad \gamma > 1$$
Net individual payoff under environmental harshness $H(t)$ and learning cost $C_i$:
$$\Pi_i(t) = z(T_{i,t}) - H(t) - C_i$$

### Patent Citation Dynamics (Preferential Attachment on Recipes)
When an agent creates a new patent $T_{\text{new}}$:
1. With probability $1 - \mu_{\text{comb}}$, it performs **parametric refinement** on an existing patent $T_j$ selected via prestige/citation weighting:
   $$P(\text{select } T_j) \propto (\text{Citations}_j)^\beta$$
2. With probability $\mu_{\text{comb}}$, it performs **combinatorial recombination** by interfacing two distinct patents $T_j$ and $T_\ell$, creating a new node that adds citations to both $j$ and $\ell$:
   $$\text{Citations}_j \leftarrow \text{Citations}_j + 1, \quad \text{Citations}_\ell \leftarrow \text{Citations}_\ell + 1$$

This naturally generates:
- **Bentley et al. (2004) / Yule power-law patent citation distributions**.
- **Endogenous super-exponential technological accumulation** during benign periods.
- **Resilience to environmental collapse**, since modular knowledge can be reconstituted even if surface variants are temporarily abandoned.
